A record of everything that has lived in this terrain and been looked at closely.

Each drawing is made from that specimen's own measurements — how much it carried, how far it reached, how many others it could hold on to, how long it lasted. Nothing is drawn from imagination, and no two look alike because no two sets of numbers are alike.

The groupings are not ours either. A classifying agent called the Namer watches what each specimen does, decides for itself what kinds of thing are here, and names them. Its words appear below exactly as it wrote them — they are the finding, not a summary of one.

Each entry opens with a plain description. Those sentences are built by fixed rules from the specimen's own measurements — the same numbers the drawing uses — so they describe what was recorded and never guess. They say what a specimen was like and how it made its living; they never say what KIND of thing it was. That judgement belongs to the Namer, and its word for it is the heading above each group.

Where a specimen shows an empty frame, it died before this terrain began keeping remains, and nothing survives to draw from.

How to read an entry

Every figure below is a measurement the terrain recorded. None of them have units — they only mean something next to each other, so each one is also described in plain words against every other specimen on record.

reach
How far out it can gather light from the ground around it. A long reach collects from a wider area but costs more to keep up.
holds N links
How many other specimens it can be joined to at once. Light moves along those joins — sometimes given, sometimes taken.
carries
How much substance it has. More lets it store more light and resist having light pulled off it by others, and costs more every shift to hold together.
makes its living by
Where its light actually comes from. ground is the cover layer growing where it stands; remains is what dead specimens left behind; others is light drawn along links from other living things.
upkeep
What it must spend every shift simply to keep existing. If it cannot cover this, it dies. Everything else has to be earned on top of it.
can hold
The most light it can store at once. Anything beyond this is lost.
lasted
How many shifts it was alive. A shift is one tick of the terrain.

The drawing is built from those same numbers: the core is what it carries, the spokes are its links, their length is its reach, the rings are offspring, and the disc beneath is how much it lived off remains.
