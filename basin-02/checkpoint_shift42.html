Result

The prediction held. Part of the finding is dismantled.

Own-history reasoning fell from 56% to 16% as the population grew fourteenfold. That component was substantially an artifact of a small population recirculating through the Namer's 12-record window — exactly as predicted before the shifts were run. What survived the same growth is narrower, and stronger for it.

The prediction, made in advance

The Namer sees a rolling window of the 12 most recent specimen records. Right now the population is small enough that the same specimens keep recurring in that window — which naturally invites continuity language. If the effect is an artifact of the small population, own-history reasoning should fall as the population grows.

The threshold was set at 180 entries — BASIN-01's early-era sample size exactly, so the comparison would be like-for-like on n. The terrain reached 183 entries at shift 42, with the population up from 18 to 241.

What was dismantled, and what was not

Dismantled: reasoning about a specimen's own history. It tracked population size almost perfectly downward. When the Namer's window was full of familiar specimens it wrote about their trajectories; when the window filled with strangers, it largely stopped. That is a property of the window, not of the Namer.

Survived, at fourteen times the population:

  • Not one entry in 183 was purely classificatory. Zero percent across every band. In BASIN-01's non-living era, 75% were.
  • Ancestry is used to argue alikeness, not merely repeated from the observation. It appears inside the comparison clause — where the case for more-alike or less-alike is actually made — in 88% of BASIN-02 entries and 80% of BASIN-01's living-era entries.
  • Every entry reasons against other named specimens. 100% across the last three bands, always inside a comparison rather than as a bare exemplar list.

Correction to this analysis

The first pass at the ancestry test reported 0% — that was a broken pattern, not a result. It truncated each comparison clause at the first period, and the comparisons are full of decimal measurements, so almost every clause was cut off after a few words. The corrected test gives 88%. The wrong number was never carried into a conclusion, but it is recorded here because a register that only publishes its successful passes is not a register.

The finding, as it now stands

Persistence is the variable — not field versus line

The primary evidence is within BASIN-01, not across the two terrains: same terrain, same Namer, same prompt, same model, with only the definition of a specimen changing at shift 49.

BASIN-02's role is corroboration with one explanation removed. It inherited BASIN-01's code but none of its history, and its Namer reasons relationally from the first specimen onward. That rules out "the Namer needed 48 shifts to adapt" — there was nothing to adapt from. It is not a second, independent discovery of the same thing, and describing it as one would overstate what two terrains can show.

Why this is not territorial behaviour

Lateral position carries no light, so a clump cannot be explained by the gradient — which makes it genuinely spatial. But the likely cause is mechanical rather than behavioural: descendants arise in their parent's own cell, and nothing in the physics pushes anything sideways. Lineages simply stay where they were born.

The spacing measurement settles it. If specimens were dispersing to avoid crowding, mean nearest-neighbour distance would grow. It fell, from 0.67 steps to 0.21. They are getting more crowded, not holding ground. On the question the field was built to ask, this is a clean negative so far.

Standing

  • Both terrains continue unchanged. No reconfiguration is warranted or taken.
  • The register keeps running passively on both, unmodified.
  • Open: whether the lateral clump is only birth-in-place. Testable by comparing where descendants end up against where they were born.
  • Open: whether ancestry-as-discriminator survives a much larger taxonomy, where categories rather than parentage may carry the comparison.
