## The software — Apache License 2.0

Every `.py` file in this repository: the shift loop, the terrain physics, the
agent roles, the write path, the page generators, the verification suite.

Apache-2.0 rather than MIT because it carries an explicit patent grant. That
costs a contributor nothing and removes a category of ambiguity for anyone who
wants to build on this.

## The governance documents — CC BY 4.0

`physics.md`, `README.md`, `OPERATING.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, and everything under
`docs/` — the Founding Charter, the Stewardship Protocol, the Classification
Standard, the Shift Log Policy, the Terrain Registry, the Physics document.

These are the part of this project most worth copying. The method — declaring a
falsification condition before the experiment runs, and binding the operator
against correcting what comes out of it — is not specific to this terrain and
does not need to be. Attribution is the only condition.

## The terrain record — CC0 1.0

Everything under `basin-*/state/` and `basin-*/shifts/`: the specimen logs, the
anomaly logs, the shift logs, the taxonomy the Namer authored, and each
terrain's memory.

The per-shift **field logs** are not in this repository — 133 MB of them would
not fit, and nothing in the project reads one back. They are archived instead at
**[10.5281/zenodo.22039882](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22039882)**, under the same CC0
dedication, with SHA-256 checksums and the schema written out. They are kept,
not discarded: they cannot be recovered by re-running, because the harness
changed in August 2026 and a fresh run no longer produces the same field.

This is dedicated to the public domain, and the reason is verification.

A record that claims to be falsifiable has to be checkable by someone who does
not trust us. Any claim made on the published pages should be independently
testable against the raw logs, and requiring permission or attribution to do
that would put friction in exactly the wrong place. If a number on this site is
wrong, we would rather someone found out than needed our consent to look.

There is a second reason, and it is a matter of honesty rather than generosity.
Under current US law, output generated by a machine without human authorship is
not copyrightable. The Namer's classifications and the Generators' substrates
are machine output. Asserting rights over them would mean claiming something we
may not hold. CC0 states plainly what is most likely already true.

**No warranty.** The record is published as it was produced, including its
errors. See `docs/` and the repository history for known defects — an audit in
August 2026 found substantial faults in the physics, in the reporting layer, and
in the harness, and several are documented in the record rather than removed
from it. Deleting an inconvenient record is forbidden by Founding Charter
Section 3, so the mistakes stay where they fell.

## Provenance

The terrain logs are append-only and are committed to git after every shift.
That history — timestamped, and not quietly rewritable — is the provenance for
every claim made about what these terrains produced. It is the reason a
per-shift commit is a standing obligation rather than a habit.
